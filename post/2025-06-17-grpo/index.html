Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

  • Xiaotian Han
· June 17, 2025

Objective Function

GRPO [1] maximizes the expected total reward of a group of responses. Given a question 𝑞 sampled from dataset 𝒟︀ and a group of 𝐺 responses {𝑜𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺 sampled from the old policy 𝜋𝜃old, the objective is:

𝒥︀(𝜃)=𝔼{(𝑞,𝑎)∼𝒟︀,{𝑜𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺∼𝜋𝜃old(⋅|𝑞)}[1𝐺∑𝑖=1𝐺1|𝑜𝑖|∑𝑡=1|𝑜𝑖|[min(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡,clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀,1+𝜀)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡)]]

where:

  • (𝑞,𝑎)∼𝒟︀ is the question-answer pair from dataset 𝒟︀
  • 𝐺 is the group size; 𝑜𝑖 is the 𝑖-th response; |𝑜𝑖| is the length of the 𝑖-th response
  • 𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)=𝜋𝜃(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡)𝜋𝜃old(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡) is the probability ratio of the 𝑡-th token in the 𝑖-th response
  • 𝜋𝜃(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡) / 𝜋𝜃old(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡) is the probability of generating token 𝑜𝑖,𝑡 under current/old policy (𝜋𝜃 / 𝜋𝜃old)
  • 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡 is the advantage of the 𝑡-th token in the 𝑖-th response

GRPO maximizes the expected total reward of the group. It:

  • increases probability of high-advantage responses
  • decreases probability of low-advantage responses
  • keeps the policy update within a trust region

Probability Ratio 𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)

The probability ratio measures how much the current policy differs from the old policy for each token:

𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)=𝜋𝜃(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡)𝜋𝜃old(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡)

where:

  • 𝜋𝜃(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡): probability of generating token 𝑜𝑖,𝑡 under current policy (∈[0,1])
  • 𝜋𝜃old(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡): probability under old policy (∈[0,1])
  • 𝑜𝑖,<𝑡: sequence of tokens before position 𝑡 in response 𝑖 (token sequence of length 𝑡−1)

Advantage 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡

The advantage of the 𝑖-th response is calculated by normalizing the group-level rewards {𝑅𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺:

𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡=𝑅𝑖−mean({𝑅𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺)std({𝑅𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺)

where:

  • 𝑅𝑖: reward for the 𝑖-th response (∈{0,1})
  • mean({𝑅𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺)=1𝐺∑𝑗=1𝐺𝑅𝑗: average reward across group
  • std({𝑅𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺): standard deviation of group rewards
  • 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡: normalized advantage for token 𝑡 in response 𝑖

Note that 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡 is computed at the response level (using response reward 𝑅𝑖), so for all tokens in the same response, 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡 is the same.

The Clipping Mechanism

The core of GRPO (inherited from PPO [2]) is the clipped objective:

min(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡,clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀,1+𝜀)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡)

Recall from the probability ratio and advantage definitions that:

𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)∈[0,+∞)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡∈{−,+}

The behavior depends on the sign of the advantage. The following table shows the raw analysis:

𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)ObjectiveClip?Prevent what?
>0(0,1−𝜀)(1−𝜀)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡Yesno/less advantage on positive
>0[1−𝜀,1+𝜀]𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡Nonormal
>0(1+𝜀,+∞)(1+𝜀)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡Yesover advantage on positive
<0(0,1−𝜀)(1−𝜀)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡Yesno/less penalty on negative
<0[1−𝜀,1+𝜀]𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡Nonormal
<0(1+𝜀,+∞)(1+𝜀)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡Yesover penalty on negative

Taking the min in the clipped objective simplifies the behavior. When 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡>0, the min effectively computes min(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀,1+𝜀)). When 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡<0, it becomes max(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀,1+𝜀)):

𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡Effective𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)ResultPrevent what?
>0min(𝑟,clip(𝑟,1−𝜀,1+𝜀))(0,1−𝜀)𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)already safe
>0min(𝑟,clip(𝑟,1−𝜀,1+𝜀))(1−𝜀,1+𝜀)𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)trust region
>0min(𝑟,clip(𝑟,1−𝜀,1+𝜀))(1+𝜀,+∞)1+𝜀over-increase
<0max(𝑟,clip(𝑟,1−𝜀,1+𝜀))(0,1−𝜀)1−𝜀over-decrease
<0max(𝑟,clip(𝑟,1−𝜀,1+𝜀))(1−𝜀,1+𝜀)𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)trust region
<0max(𝑟,clip(𝑟,1−𝜀,1+𝜀))(1+𝜀,+∞)𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)self-penalizing

Intuition:

  • If 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡>0 and 𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)>1+𝜀: the model already has higher advantage on this case. To prevent over-increase, clip 𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃) to 1+𝜀.
  • If 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡<0 and 𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)<1−𝜀: the model already has lower advantage on this case. To prevent over-decrease, clip 𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃) to 1−𝜀.

As stated in the original PPO paper: “We only ignore the change in probability ratio when it would make the objective improve, and we include it when it makes the objective worse.”

Follow-ups and Variants

GRPO in DeepSeek R1

DeepSeek R1 [3] modifies the original GRPO objective which includes a length normalization term 1|𝑜𝑖| and a KL divergence penalty:

𝒥︀(𝜃)=𝔼{(𝑞,𝑎)∼𝒟︀,{𝑜𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺∼𝜋𝜃old(⋅|𝑞)}[1𝐺∑𝑖=1𝐺1|𝑜𝑖|∑𝑡=1|𝑜𝑖|[min(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡,clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀,1+𝜀)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡)−𝛽𝐷KL]]

DeepSeek R1 removes the length normalization term 1|𝑜𝑖|:

𝒥︀(𝜃)=𝔼{(𝑞,𝑎)∼𝒟︀,{𝑜𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺∼𝜋𝜃old(⋅|𝑞)}[1𝐺∑𝑖=1𝐺∑𝑡=1|𝑜𝑖|[min(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡,clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀,1+𝜀)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡)−𝛽𝐷KL]]

Dr. GRPO

Dr. GRPO [4] (GRPO Done Right, without bias) removes both the length normalization and the standard deviation normalization:

𝒥︀Dr. GRPO(𝜃)=𝔼{(𝑞,𝑎)∼𝒟︀,{𝑜𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺∼𝜋𝜃old(⋅|𝑞)}[1𝐺∑𝑖=1𝐺∑𝑡=1|𝑜𝑖|min(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡,clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀,1+𝜀)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡)],

where 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡=𝑅𝑖−mean({𝑅𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺) (no std normalization compared to the original GRPO advantage).

DAPO

Decouple Clip and Dynamic sAmpling Policy Optimization (DAPO) [5] introduces several modifications:

𝒥︀DAPO(𝜃)=𝔼{(𝑞,𝑎)∼𝒟︀,{𝑜𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺∼𝜋𝜃old(⋅|𝑞)}[1∑𝑖=1𝐺|𝑜𝑖|∑𝑖=1𝐺∑𝑡=1|𝑜𝑖|min(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡,clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀low,1+𝜀high)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡)]s.t.0<|{𝑜𝑖|is_equivalent(𝑎,𝑜𝑖)}|<𝐺,

Key innovations:

  • Clip-Higher: asymmetric clipping with separate 𝜀low and 𝜀high, raising the ceiling
  • Dynamic sampling: over-sample and filter out prompts with the accuracy equal to 1 and 0
  • Token-level loss: rebalancing with token-level policy gradient loss

GPG

Group Policy Gradient (GPG) [6] uses log-probabilities directly instead of importance sampling ratios:

𝒥︀GPG(𝜃)=𝔼{(𝑞,𝑎)∼𝒟︀,{𝑜𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺}[1∑𝑖=1𝐺|𝑜𝑖|∑𝑖=1𝐺∑𝑡=1|𝑜𝑖|(log𝜋𝜃(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡)],

where 𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡=𝑅𝑖−mean({𝑅𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺)𝐹norm with 𝐹norm being an optional normalization technique.

CISPO

Clipped IS-weight Policy Optimization (CISPO) [7] combines importance sampling weights with log-probability gradients:

𝒥︀CISPO(𝜃)=𝔼{(𝑞,𝑎)∼𝒟︀,{𝑜𝑖}𝑖=1𝐺∼𝜋𝜃old(⋅|𝑞)}[1∑𝑖=1𝐺|𝑜𝑖|∑𝑖=1𝐺∑𝑡=1|𝑜𝑖|sg(𝑟̂𝑖,𝑡(𝜃))𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡log𝜋𝜃(𝑜𝑖,𝑡|𝑞,𝑜𝑖,<𝑡)],

where 𝑟̂𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)=clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀lowIS,1+𝜀highIS) is the clipped IS weight and sg(⋅) denotes stop-gradient.

C3PO

Constrained Contextual Computation Policy Optimization (C3PO) [8] introduces a training token budget constraint:

ℒ︀C3PO(𝜃)=𝔼{{𝑞,𝑎}𝑙=1𝐿∼𝒟︀,{𝑦𝑖}𝑖=1𝐾∼𝜋old(⋅|𝑞)}[1Φ∑𝑖=1𝑆∑𝑡=1|𝑦𝑖|𝕀[𝑦𝑖,𝑡∈Ψ](min(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴𝑖,𝑡,clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀,1+𝜀)𝐴𝑖,𝑡)−𝛽𝐷KL))],

subject to |Ψ|=Φ, where:

Ψ={(𝑦1,𝑦2,…,𝑦𝑁)|𝑦𝑖∈ℬ︀}s.t.𝑁≤|ℬ︀|,∑𝑗=1𝑁−1|𝑦𝑗|<Φ,∑𝑗=1𝑁|𝑦𝑗|≥Φ

and:

  • Φ is the training token budget
  • Ψ is the selected tokens by custom sampling strategy
  • 𝑆 is the selected responses for training
  • 𝑦𝑖 is the 𝑖-th response

Value-Based Variants

PPO

A response trajectory 𝜏=(𝑠0,𝑎0,𝑟1,…,𝑠𝑡,𝑎𝑡,𝑟𝑡+1,…,𝑠𝑇−1,𝑎𝑇−1,𝑟𝑇,𝑠𝑇). PPO [2] optimizes the total rewards:

𝒥︀PPO(𝜃)=𝔼𝑡,𝑠𝑡,𝑎𝑡∼𝜋𝜃old[min(𝑟𝑡𝐴̂𝑡,clip(𝑟𝑡,1−𝜀,1+𝜀)𝐴̂𝑡)]

where the advantage is estimated via Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE):

𝐴̂𝑡=𝛿𝑡+(𝛾𝜆)𝛿𝑡+1+(𝛾𝜆)2𝛿𝑡+2+…+(𝛾𝜆)𝑇−𝑡−1𝛿𝑇−1𝛿𝑡=𝑟𝑡+1+𝛾𝑉(𝑠𝑡+1)−𝑉(𝑠𝑡)

Here 𝛿𝑡 is the Temporal Difference (TD) error between the estimated value of the next state 𝑟𝑡+1+𝛾𝑉(𝑠𝑡+1) and the estimated value of the current state 𝑉(𝑠𝑡).

VC-PPO

Value-Calibrated PPO (VC-PPO) [9] uses the same PPO objective, and addresses the value initialization bias through:

  • Value-Pretraining: addressing the value initialization bias by value pretraining
  • Decoupled-GAE: improving in-training value estimate with decoupled-GAE

VAPO

Value-model-based Augmented PPO (VAPO) [10] optimizes:

ℒ︀VAPO(𝜃)=[1∑𝑖=1𝐺|𝑜𝑖|∑𝑖=1𝐺∑𝑡=1|𝑜𝑖|min(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡,clip(𝑟𝑖,𝑡(𝜃),1−𝜀low,1+𝜀high)𝐴̂𝑖,𝑡)]

Key innovations:

  • Length-Adaptive GAE: address the inconsistency across sequences of varying lengths
  • Token-level policy gradient loss

  • Dealing with sparsity of reward signal in verifier-based tasks:

    • Clip-higher
    • Positive example LM loss: additional negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss for the correct outcomes
    • Group-sampling: sample discriminative positive and negative samples within the same prompt

T-PPO

Truncated Proximal Policy Optimization (T-PPO) [11] optimizes:

𝒥︀T-PPO(𝜃)=𝔼𝑡,𝑠𝑡,𝑎𝑡∼𝜋𝜃old[min(𝜋𝜃(𝑎𝑡|𝑠𝑡)𝜋𝜃old(𝑎𝑡|𝑠𝑡)𝐴̂𝑡,clip(𝜋𝜃(𝑎𝑡|𝑠𝑡)𝜋𝜃old(𝑎𝑡|𝑠𝑡),1−𝜀low,1+𝜀high)𝐴̂𝑡)]

Key innovations:

  • Extended GAE: improved advantage estimation
  • Token filtering: if some sequences reach an ending condition, these sequences are removed in the next training step
