Logit Gradient Correction: Deriving Score Centering from Logit Gradients

  • Xiaotian Han
· September 24, 2026 ·PDF

This post derives SCORE CENTERING from removing a bias term in the logit gradient before it is propagated through the logit Jacobian via chain rule, and then derives the corresponding surrogate objective.

This method is introduced in Score Centering Stabilizes Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning, and the perspective presented here is inspired by this discussion.

Background: REINFORCE and the Logit Gradient

At a fixed prefix 𝒙, a trainer with 𝐷 parameters 𝜽∈ℝ𝐷 produces logits 𝒛(𝒙,𝜽)∈ℝ𝑉 over a vocabulary of size 𝑉. Its probability vector 𝒑=softmax(𝒛)∈ℝ𝑉 defines the trainer distribution 𝑃, with 𝑃(𝑎)=𝑝𝑎. A sampler at the same prefix generates token 𝑎∼𝑄, with probabilities 𝒒∈ℝ𝑉 and 𝑄(𝑎)=𝑞𝑎. During backward, the recorded 𝒒 and reward 𝑅𝑎∈ℝ are held fixed. Scalars use plain letters, vectors bold lowercase, and matrices bold uppercase.

REINFORCE wants to increase the trainer’s expected reward 𝐽𝑃 under the trainer’s own distribution 𝑃:

𝐽𝑃=𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝑅𝑎]=∑𝑎𝑝𝑎𝑅𝑎.

Holding the reward function fixed, differentiate 𝐽𝑃 with respect to 𝜽 to obtain ∇𝜽𝐽𝑃∈ℝ𝐷:

∇𝜽𝐽𝑃=∑𝑎𝑅𝑎∇𝜽𝑝𝑎=∑𝑎𝑝𝑎𝑅𝑎∇𝜽log𝑝𝑎=𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝑅𝑎∇𝜽log𝑝𝑎].

The term 𝑅𝑎∇𝜽log𝑝𝑎∈ℝ𝐷 is REINFORCE’s per-sample gradient contribution. With 𝑅𝑎 held fixed, it equals the negative gradient with respect to 𝜽 of the reward-weighted cross-entropy loss −𝑅𝑎log𝑝𝑎.

We first differentiate log𝑝𝑎 with respect to the logits 𝒛. Given that 𝒑=softmax(𝒛), we have

Same to ∇𝒛CE(𝒆𝑎,softmax(𝒛))=𝒑−𝒆𝑎∈ℝ𝑉.

∇𝒛log𝑝𝑎=𝒆𝑎−𝒑∈ℝ𝑉.

Here, 𝒆𝑎∈ℝ𝑉 is the sampled token’s one-hot vector. The gradient 𝒆𝑎−𝒑 contains the derivatives of log𝑝𝑎 with respect to the logits of all vocabulary tokens. The mean logits contribution on the right therefore determines the mean parameter contribution.

Log-Prob Gradients Have Zero/Nonzero Bias when On/off-Policy

We consider a scenario that 𝑅𝑎=1 for every token, we have:

𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝑅𝑎∇𝒛log𝑝𝑎]=𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[∇𝒛log𝑝𝑎]=𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝒆𝑎−𝒑]=𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝒆𝑎]−𝒑∈ℝ𝑉.

Thus the gradient of 𝐽𝑃 with respect to the model parameters 𝜽 will be

∇𝜽𝐽𝑃=𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝑅𝑎∇𝜽log𝑝𝑎]=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝑅𝑎∇𝒛log𝑝𝑎]=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤(𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝒆𝑎]−𝒑)∈ℝ𝐷×𝑉×ℝ𝑉=ℝ𝐷.

The matrix 𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽∈ℝ𝑉×𝐷 is the trainer’s logit Jacobian: it describes how the logits change with the model parameters. Its transpose maps a 𝑉-dimensional logits gradient to a 𝐷-dimensional parameter gradient, applying the chain rule through backpropagation. At a fixed prefix and parameter value, this matrix is the same for every possible sampled token, so it can be moved outside the expectation. Score Centering supplies a corrected logits gradient to this same backward pass.

If the tokens are generated by the trainer distribution 𝑃, the expectation 𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝒆𝑎] equals 𝒑,

∇𝜽𝐽𝑃=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤(𝔼𝑎∼𝑃[𝒆𝑎]−𝒑)=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤(𝒑−𝒑)=𝟎.

the logits gradient is unbiased and has zero mean when the reward is constant.

Logit Gradient Correction

If tokens instead come from the sampler distribution 𝑄, then 𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝒆𝑎]=𝒒. Still assuming 𝑅𝑎=1, the mean trainer log-probability gradient becomes:

𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[∇𝜽log𝑝𝑎]=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤(𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝒆𝑎]−𝒑)=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤(𝒒−𝒑)∈ℝ𝐷.

Here 𝑄 specifies where tokens come from; the differentiated log-probability still belongs to 𝑃. This expression is the mean sampled contribution, not the gradient of the sampler’s expected reward.

Since every reward is 1, the trainer objective 𝐽𝑃=1 has zero gradient. We want the corrected contributions to have zero mean even when tokens come from 𝑄. Shift each one-hot contribution 𝒆𝑎 by 𝒑−𝒒, giving 𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝒆𝑎−𝒒+𝒑]=𝒒−𝒒+𝒑=𝒑.

Let 𝐽 denote the per-sample surrogate objective that we will construct in the next section. We require its expected parameter gradient to equal the corrected mean contribution. With 𝑄 held fixed during backward, this requirement is:

∇𝜽𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝐽]=𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[∇𝜽𝐽]=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤(𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝒆𝑎−𝒒+𝒑]−𝒑)=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤(𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝒆𝑎−𝒒+𝒑−𝒑])=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤(𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝒆𝑎−𝒒])=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤(𝒒−𝒒)=𝟎.

The essential of this correction is to replace each token’s logits gradient contribution 𝒆𝑎−𝒑 with 𝒆𝑎−𝒒. Averaging under the sampler distribution 𝑄 then gives ∑𝑎𝑞𝑎(𝒆𝑎−𝒒)=𝟎, restoring zero mean for constant rewards.

From Corrected Logit Gradient to a Surrogate Objective

We now construct the per-sample surrogate 𝐽. For constant reward 𝑅𝑎=1, the required relation is:

∇𝜽𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝐽]=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝒆𝑎−𝒒].

For varying rewards, we weight each corrected contribution by 𝑅𝑎. The required gradient of the expected surrogate is

∇𝜽𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝐽]=(𝜕𝒛𝜕𝜽)⊤𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝑅𝑎(𝒆𝑎−𝒒)].

First, we seek a scalar surrogate objective 𝐽 in terms of the logits 𝒛, and whose gradient with respect to the logits 𝒛 is:

∇𝒛𝐽=𝑅𝑎(𝒆𝑎−𝒒)∈ℝ𝑉.

The required gradient 𝑅𝑎(𝒆𝑎−𝒒) is constant with respect to 𝒛. An intuitive antiderivative is a linear function whose gradient equals its coefficient vector:

𝐽=𝑅𝑎(𝒆𝑎−𝒒)⊤𝒛=𝑅𝑎(𝒆𝑎⊤𝒛−∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣]𝑧𝑣)=𝑅𝑎(𝑧𝑎−∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣]𝑧𝑣).

The last line uses 𝒆𝑎⊤𝒛=𝑧𝑎. The sum ∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣]𝑧𝑣 is the sampler-weighted average of the trainer’s logits over the entire vocabulary. The operator sg holds the probabilities fixed during differentiation while preserving gradients through the logits.

Second, we express the surrogate objective 𝐽 in terms of log-probabilities. Softmax gives log𝑝𝑣=𝑧𝑣−log(∑𝑗exp(𝑧𝑗)) for every token 𝑣. Using ∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣]=1, we obtain:

𝑧𝑎−∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣]𝑧𝑣=(𝑧𝑎−log(∑𝑗exp(𝑧𝑗)))−[∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣]𝑧𝑣−log(∑𝑗exp(𝑧𝑗))]=(𝑧𝑎−log(∑𝑗exp(𝑧𝑗)))−[∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣]𝑧𝑣−(∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣])⏟=1log(∑𝑗exp(𝑧𝑗))]=(𝑧𝑎−log(∑𝑗exp(𝑧𝑗)))−∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣](𝑧𝑣−log(∑𝑗exp(𝑧𝑗)))=log𝑝𝑎−∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣]log𝑝𝑣.

We have the surrogate objective in terms of log-probability:

𝐽=𝑅𝑎[log𝑝𝑎−∑𝑣sg[𝑞𝑣]log𝑝𝑣]=𝑅𝑎[log𝑝𝑎−𝔼𝑣∼𝑄[log𝑝𝑣]].

The surrogate loss replaces the per-sample REINFORCE objective 𝑅𝑎log𝑝𝑎 with 𝑅𝑎 times the difference between the sampled token’s trainer log-probability and the 𝑄-weighted average of the trainer’s log-probabilities over the vocabulary.

Averaging the per-sample surrogate over tokens drawn from 𝑄 gives:

𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝐽]=𝔼𝑎∼𝑄[𝑅𝑎[log𝑝𝑎−𝔼𝑣∼𝑄[log𝑝𝑣]]].
